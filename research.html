Research · Doctoral journey

From human performance to brain signals.

Understanding the mind through data.

My doctoral research began with the automatic assessment of drivers’ ability to operate vehicles. That starting point led me toward physiological signals, the interplay of attention and emotion, and eventually the role of artificial intelligence in understanding mental health.

Today, my work increasingly centers on EEG-based depression detection and the methodological questions involved in translating brain-signal patterns into meaningful, reliable models.

Research perspective

A changing question.
A clearer direction.

What began as an engineering question about driver safety developed into a broader investigation of how the body and brain respond to cognitive and affective demands. Early work examined driver monitoring, electroencephalography (EEG), electrocardiography (ECG), galvanic skin response (GSR), and the processing of physiological data.

A later stage shifted toward EEG-based analysis of affective states, with a particular emphasis on depression. This direction combines biomedical signal processing, machine learning, and critical appraisal of the evidence needed to develop reliable AI-assisted assessment approaches.
